1
Benchmark dominance: 77.1% ARC-AGI2 — but tools change the story Naked reasoning king, but Opus 4.6 catches up & pulls ahead when equipped with tools
▾

Gemini 3.1 Pro leads 13/16 benchmarks. The 46-point ARC-AGI2 jump in 90 days is the largest single-generation reasoning gain any frontier model family has ever produced. But when you add web search, code execution, and database access, Opus 4.6 catches up and often pulls ahead — scoring 53.1% vs Gemini’s 51.4% on Humanity’s Last Exam with tools, and leading by 289 ELO on GDPVal (expert office & financial tasks).

Naked benchmarks

  • ARC-AGI2: 77.1% (Opus 68.8%, GPT 5.2 lower)
  • GPQA Diamond (science): near-saturation scores
  • Live Codebench Pro: very strong coding marks
  • Configurable thinking: low / medium / high / max

Equipped (with tools)

  • Humanity’s Last Exam + tools: Opus leads
  • GDPVal (office/finance): Opus +289 ELO gap
  • Arena coding + human preference: Claude wins
  • Pattern: naked ≠ equipped
Key insight: “Google built a better engine. Anthropic built a better car. OpenAI built a better racing transmission.”
2
Google’s vertical stack: from transistor design to Nobel Prizes $100B+ free cash flow • Ironwood TPUs • competitors train on Google’s hardware
▾

Google controls the full stack: custom Ironwood TPUs (7th gen, 10x compute, half energy), Google Cloud (used by 9/10 AI research labs), 650M+ monthly Gemini users, and DeepMind’s Nobel-winning AlphaFold. Anthropic signed a multi-billion-dollar deal for a million TPUs. Meta is negotiating similar. When your competitors train on your hardware, you have built something beyond a moat.

The stack

  • Silicon: Ironwood TPU, 9,216-chip pods
  • Cloud: 9/10 AI labs use Google Cloud
  • Distribution: 650M Gemini + search + Android
  • Research: DeepMind → Nobel Prize (AlphaFold)

Why nobody else can replicate this

  • Microsoft: no chips, fragmented Copilot UX
  • Amazon: AWS + Trainium chips, but models trail frontier
  • Meta: research + social, but no cloud or chip stack
  • Anthropic: best agentic product, but runs on others’ hardware
Flywheel: Jeff Dean is using AI to shrink TPU design cycles from 2 years to 6–9 months. Intelligence building the hardware that runs intelligence.
3
Three model archetypes: naked reasoner, equipped reasoner, specialist coder Gemini = engine • Opus = car • Codex = racing transmission
▾

The model landscape has clearly differentiated. Gemini 3.1 Pro is the strongest naked reasoner — pure thinking, novel logic, first-principles deduction. Opus 4.6 is the strongest equipped reasoner — combining intelligence with tools, APIs, file systems, and sustained work over hours and days. GPT 5.3/Codex is the strongest specialist coder with self-bootstrapping sandboxes and thousand-token/sec throughput.

When to pick each

  • Gemini 3.1 Pro: novel logic, multi-step deduction, science, math
  • Opus 4.6: agentic work, tool use, multi-day coding, team coordination
  • GPT 5.3 / Codex: specialized coding pipelines, throughput-critical tasks

The real question

  • “Which model is smartest?” → wrong question
  • “Is the bottleneck raw thinking or acting on thinking?” → right question
  • Model routing is now a skill — domain-specific, compounds weekly
Practical: model routing is the gap between commodity AI usage and actual leverage. Nobody will build that routing for your domain except you.
4
Six types of “hard”: why benchmarks only measure one axis Reasoning • Effort • Coordination • Emotional intelligence • Judgment • Ambiguity
▾

We’ve been treating “hard work” as one thing. It’s not. Each dimension is getting automated on a different timeline by different tools. Pure reasoning is ~10% of most knowledge work. The other 90% is effort, coordination, emotional intelligence, judgment, and ambiguity — and those map to very different models (or no model at all).

AI-tractable axes (automating now)

  • Reasoning: multi-jurisdiction tax, derivative pricing, fraud tracing → Gemini on max
  • Effort: 3K vendor audits, 2M-line migrations, churn reviews → agentic Opus / Codex
  • Coordination: aligning 6 teams, routing dependencies, org awareness → Opus agent teams

Human-dominant axes (not automating soon)

  • Emotional IQ: feedback during divorce, reading boardroom silence
  • Judgment / Willpower: killing a 6-month project, saying no to lucrative client
  • Ambiguity: contradictory market signals, what does the customer actually want?
The 10/90 split: the tax attorney spends 10% on the genuine multi-jurisdiction reasoning puzzle — and 90% on client management, coordination, and navigating ambiguity. A better engine helps the 10%. A better car helps the 90%.
5
Demis Hassabis’s mission: “Solve intelligence, then solve everything else” Deep Think • 18 unsolved problems • AGI within 5 years • the product race is a sideshow
▾

Hassabis has repeated the same sentence for 15 years — from when DeepMind was an unknown London startup, through AlphaGo, through the Nobel Prize, through Davos. Google isn’t trying to win the product race. They think it’s a sideshow. The main event is intelligence itself. Deep Think collaborated with researchers to solve 18 previously unsolved problems — disproving a 2015 conjecture, crossing disciplinary boundaries humans rarely cross.

Deep Think breakthroughs

  • Disproved a 2015 online submodular optimization conjecture
  • Solved max-cut via Krivine theorem + measure theory (cross-disciplinary)
  • Caught a critical error in a cryptography paper
  • Isomorphic Labs: 2x AlphaFold 3 accuracy on hardest protein tasks

Google’s quiet game

  • $93B capex this year, mostly AI
  • Hassabis: AGI “very very soon” (updated to ~5 years)
  • Can lose the model race entirely and still be fine
  • Models are experiments in intelligence, not the business
Strategic read: Google is building “the thing underneath the thing” — the engine that disproves conjectures, discovers drug-binding sites, and pushes what “thinking” means when a machine does it.

What to do with this (action checklist)

Three moves for tomorrow

Map your work’s difficulty dimensions

  • Stop asking “which AI is best?” — start asking “which AI for which problem?”
  • Decompose your weekly tasks: what % is reasoning, effort, coordination, ambiguity, emotional IQ, judgment?
  • If most value comes from axes AI isn’t automating, you can sleep better — but route the tractable parts.
  • If most value is reasoning or effort: move deliberately toward human-judgment dimensions.

Build model-routing skill + output taste

  • Route financial modeling to Gemini on high, coding to Claude Code, quick research to Gemini Flash, deep doc analysis to Opus.
  • The gap between “I use ChatGPT for everything” and domain-specific routing is the difference between commodity usage and actual leverage.
  • Build the taste to evaluate AI output in your domain — every model improvement makes this more urgent, not less.
  • A mathematician used Deep Think to catch a flaw in peer-reviewed work. The model found it; the human had to validate it. Both steps were necessary.
Meta-lesson: the model landscape has differentiated enough that “which AI should I use?” is the wrong question. The right question: “which AI for which problem?” People who route models well will outperform those who use one model for everything — and that margin widens every month.
