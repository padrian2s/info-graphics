The Single Biggest Bottleneck to Scaling AI Compute

It's not power. It's not data centers. It's semiconductors — specifically EUV lithography tools, memory fabs, and a supply chain that tops out at ~200 GW of AI chips by 2030. Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis) maps the entire constraint stack from ASML's 70 machines/year to $600B in Big Tech CapEx.

The 5 stories that matter

Click to expand · data-heavy
1
The compute race: Anthropic was conservative, OpenAI went aggressive — and it matters Anthropic needs 5+ GW by year-end but was cautious on signing deals. OpenAI locked in early.
▾

Anthropic's revenue added $4B in January, $6B in February. Drawing a straight line = $60B in revenue over the next 10 months. At sub-50% gross margins, that's ~$40B of compute spend, which at $10B/GW rental cost means 4 GW of inference capacity just for revenue growth — on top of existing research fleet.

Anthropic's conundrum

  • Was conservative: "didn't want to go bankrupt" — Dario on Dwarkesh.
  • Now must go to lower-quality providers for last-minute compute.
  • Paying premium: H100 deals at $2.40/hr for 2-3 years (cost to build: $1.40).
  • Relying on Bedrock, Vertex, Foundry revenue-share arrangements.
  • Anthropic compute team saw Google's TPU dislocation before Google did.

OpenAI's advantage

  • Signed "crazy deals" early — CoreWeave, Oracle, SoftBank Energy, NScale.
  • 5-year contracts locked in at historical prices → huge margin advantage.
  • Went from 600 MW → 2 GW last year → 6+ GW this year → 12 GW next year.
  • Raised $110B vs Anthropic's $30B.
  • Financial freakouts in H2 2024 ("OpenAI can't pay") now resolved.
Key dynamic: GPU value goes UP over time because better models extract more value per chip. An H100 is worth more today than 3 years ago. Early commitments = locked-in margin advantage. "Commitment issues" are very costly.
2
EUV: the world's most complex machine, and why ASML can't just make more 70 tools/year → ~100 by 2030. $300-400M each. 10,000+ suppliers. Tin droplets hit by lasers 3x.
▾

1 GW of AI chips requires ~2 million EUV wafer passes. At 75 wafers/hour and 90% uptime, that's 3.5 EUV tools per gigawatt. The tools cost $1.2B for a GW, but enable $50B of data center CapEx. The bottleneck is that ASML's supply chain simply cannot scale faster.

How EUV works

  • Source: tin droplets hit by laser 3x — expand → shape → blast → emit 13.5nm light.
  • Optics: Carl Zeiss makes 18 multilayer mirrors per tool. Molybdenum + ruthenium layers.
  • Reticle stage: moves at 9 Gs in opposite direction to wafer stage.
  • Precision: sub-nanometer accuracy. Layer-to-layer overlay ~3nm.
  • Assembled in Eindhoven, deconstructed, shipped by plane, reassembled at customer site.

Why it can't scale faster

  • 10,000+ suppliers in the supply chain.
  • Carl Zeiss: <1,000 people on projection optics. Artisanal-scale.
  • Cymer source: complex supply chain in San Diego.
  • ASML is not "AGI-pilled" — they think 60 → 100 is aggressive growth.
  • Even if they wanted to 2x, the subcomponent supply chains can't follow.
ASML paradox: They have an absolute monopoly on EUV — no one else is even close — yet they haven't raised prices aggressively. "Maybe one of the most generous companies in the world." The margin is there. They just haven't taken it.
3
The memory crisis: 30% of Big Tech CapEx, iPhones $150 more expensive, smartphone sales halving HBM needs 4x more wafer area per bit than regular DRAM. Memory vendors didn't build fabs during 2023 crash.
▾

HBM4 delivers 2.5 TB/s per stack vs ~100 GB/s for DDR5 in the same area — 25x more bandwidth. That's why you can't just use commodity DRAM. But HBM uses 4x more wafer area per bit, meaning every byte going to AI destroys 4x more consumer DRAM capacity. Memory prices have tripled.

Consumer impact

  • iPhone memory cost: $50 → $150 ($100 increase).
  • Add NAND increases: total iPhone BOM up ~$150.
  • Low-end smartphones hit hardest: higher % of BOM is memory, lower margins.
  • Smartphone volumes: 1.1B → projected 800M this year, 500-600M next year.
  • Xiaomi, Oppo cutting low/mid-range volumes by half.

Why it's getting worse

  • Memory vendors lost money in 2023 — stopped building fabs.
  • Fabs take 2 years to build. Meaningful new capacity: late 2027/2028.
  • Reasoning models = long context = big KV cache = massive memory demand.
  • Nvidia is now largest customer at both TSMC and SK Hynix.
  • No more capacity to shift from mobile/PC to AI — it's tapped out.
The meme is real: "Cat dancing videos — this is why memory prices doubled and you can't get a new gaming GPU." It's going to get worse when memory prices double again.
4
Power is solvable: 16+ vendors, hundreds of GW, ship engines, fuel cells — it's not the bottleneck Energy is 10-15% of cluster cost. Doubling power cost adds $0.10/hr to GPU cost. The supply chain is simple.
▾

SemiAnalysis tracks 16+ power vendors. Combined-cycle turbines are $1,500/kW, but even at $3,500/kW for behind-the-meter alternatives, GPU hourly cost only goes from $1.40 to $1.50. The US grid is terawatt-level, and 20% is idle reserve capacity that batteries could unlock.

The power zoo

  • Combined-cycle gas turbines: 3 vendors (GE, Siemens, Mitsubishi).
  • Aeroderivatives: airplane engines → turbines (Boom Supersonic + Crusoe).
  • Reciprocating engines: 10+ vendors (Cummins etc). Diesel → gas convertible.
  • Ship engines: Nebius running them for Microsoft data center in NJ.
  • Fuel cells: Bloom Energy, fast payback, scalable production.
  • Solar + battery: cost curves still falling.

Why power isn't the constraint

  • Each source individually does tens of GW. Combined: hundreds of GW.
  • Half of new capacity will be behind the meter by end of decade.
  • Data centers: 3-4% of US grid today → 10% by 2028. Grid is TW-scale.
  • 20% grid reserve could be unlocked with utility-scale batteries.
  • Data centers can be built in 8 months (Amazon). Fabs take 2-3 years.
Patel's hierarchy: Power and data centers are shorter lead-time, simpler supply chains. The real bottleneck is the semiconductor supply chain — fabs (2-3 year build), EUV tools (artisanal), and memory (no new fabs since 2023).
5
2030 outlook: space is premature, China catches up on long timelines, chips remain king Space data centers lose 6 months of deployment time. China gets DUV by 2030, EUV working but not scaled.
▾

Elon wants 100 GW/year in space by 2028-29. Patel's counter: in a chip-constrained world, you want GPUs producing tokens the moment they're manufactured. Space adds 6 months of deployment delay = 10% of a GPU's useful life. Networking between satellites is orders of magnitude worse than InfiniBand. Space makes sense once chips aren't the bottleneck — not this decade.

Why space doesn't work (yet)

  • GPUs must be tested, deconstructed, launched, reassembled — 6+ months.
  • 15% of Blackwells need RMA on deployment. Can't fix in space.
  • Satellite interconnects ~100 Gbps vs InfiniBand 400+ GB/s per GPU.
  • Higher watts/mm² = hotter chips. Harder to cool in space.
  • Energy is only 10-15% of cluster cost — free power doesn't overcome these.

China + long timelines

  • Fast US timelines → US wins (revenue compounding, compute scaling, distillation gets harder).
  • Long timelines → China catches up with indigenized supply chain.
  • China: fully indigenized DUV by 2030, EUV working but not scaled.
  • ~100 DUV tools/year vs ASML's hundreds. Closing but not equal.
  • Anthropic at $20B ARR needs $50B of CapEx behind it. China hasn't built this.
Patel's thesis: "All that matters in a chip-constrained world is getting chips producing tokens ASAP." Space, fancy cooling, or exotic architectures don't matter until chips stop being the bottleneck. That's post-2030 at earliest.

What to do with this

Pick the row that matches you

If you're an AI lab or compute buyer

  • Sign long-term compute contracts NOW. The price of GPUs goes up over time as models improve. Early commitment = margin advantage.
  • Don't be "conservative" on compute — Anthropic's caution is now costing them premium rates and lower-quality providers.
  • Memory is the near-term crunch. Plan for 3x memory costs in your financial models.
  • Power is solvable — behind-the-meter from any of 16+ vendors. Don't let power be the reason you don't build.

If you're an investor or supply chain participant

  • The semiconductor supply chain is the real bottleneck through 2030. Everything else can scale.
  • ASML, Carl Zeiss, memory vendors — these are the true chokepoints. ASML hasn't even taken their margin yet.
  • Consumer electronics will get more expensive. Smartphone volumes halving to 500-600M.
  • Fast AI timelines → US wins. Long timelines → China catches up with indigenized supply chain. Bet accordingly.
Patel's bottom line: "Everyone downstream from the semiconductor supply chain thinks they need X. Nvidia is building X - 1. The rest of the supply chain is doing X ÷ 2. Because they're not AGI-pilled." The whip hasn't cracked all the way down yet.
